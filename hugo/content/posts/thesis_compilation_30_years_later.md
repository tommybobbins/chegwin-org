# Compiling LaTeX 30 years later

Since I started working at [Overleaf](https://www.overleaf.com), I've been using my thesis as a test for checking difficult documents. The good news is that it still compiles under latex, pdflatex and under Overleaf. It just about compiles under the free-tier, so it's the perfect document for me.

I need to work on replacing the encapsulated postscripts (eps) that I used for figures. I can and will replace these with pngs, so that more of my thesis can be colour.

This has led me to revisit how many mistakes my thesis contains in terms of LaTeX, files that I left lying around, backups that emacs made, crash dumps, other people's .bib files.

I've created a new version of my thesis which has shrunk from 1.5MB of compressed files to 1M. I'm pretty pleased with this, but really I'm grinning with how robust LaTeX is 30 years later.
